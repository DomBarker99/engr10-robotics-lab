LiDAR– Light Detection and Ranging
The LiDAR instrument fires rapid pulses of laser light at a surface, some at up to 150,000 pulses per second. A sensor on the instrument measures the amount of time it takes for each pulse to bounce back. Differences in laser return times and wavelengths can then be used to make digital 3-D representations of the target.

Proximity sensors
Analog Sensors
There are 4 main types of proximity sensors
A proximity sensor is a sensor able to detect the presence of nearby objects without any physical contact. 
Inductive proximity sensors create an electromagnetic field and detects the change in the field when metallic object is brought near. used to detect the presence of metals (iron steel, aluminum, copper, …)
Capacitive proximity sensors sense "target" objects due to the target's ability to be electrically charged. Since even non-conductors can hold charges, this means that just about any object can be detected with this type of sensor (plastic, glass, wood, liquid, ….) 
Consider the following when deciding on the type of proximity sensor to use: Object, accuracy, range and distance, response time, environment, size, and cost

Ultrasonic proximity sensors detects the presence of objects through emitting high-frequency ultrasonic range. It does so through the conversion of electrical energy. Similarly to capacitive sensors, it can detect objects in solid, liquid, or granular as well.
Probably the easiest among all, it only comprises an ultrasonic transmitter and an ultrasonic receiver.
IR (infrared) proximity sensors , in short it detects the presence of an object by emitting a beam of infrared light. It works similarly to ultrasonic sensors, though instead of using sonic waves, IR is transmitted.

Blind spot detection or object avoidance: In autonomous and semi-autonomous systems.
Parking sensors: The classic use — those “beeps” when you're backing up are ultrasonic sensors detecting obstacles behind your car.
Transparent object detection: Unlike photoelectric sensors, ultrasonic sensors don’t get confused by clear plastic or glass
Fill level control: Sense how full a container is during filling operations

Binary Numbers - Bits, Bytes and Words
Consider a number 7582, it has four digits
The 2 is filling the “1s place”, the 8 is filling the “10s place”, the 5 is filling the “100s place” while the 7 is filling the “1000s place”.
(7 * 1000) + (5 * 100) + (8 * 10) + (2 * 1) = 7000 + 500 + 80 + 2 = 7582
A different way of expressing the same number is to use powers of 10 – this is a base 10 system.
(7 * 103) + (5 * 102) + (8 * 101) + (2 * 100) = 7000 + 500 + 80 + 2 = 7582
We can select any number as the base, for example 8, 5, ….
Computers operate using the base 2 number system, also known as the binary number system.

With the advances made in technological, both the serial and parallel port have largely been replaced by the use of USB ports, and fewer new devices are designed to include either a serial or parallel port.
Universal Serial Bus - USB
USB ports support much faster (often 100x or greater) data transfers than serial or parallel.
The USB industry standard exists in multiple versions including 1.1, 2.0, 3.0, 3.1 and 3.2. However, USB ports feature identical physical layouts no matter the version of USB supported.
